# Order attribution imports

The campaign report accepts a normalized JSON snapshot of paid orders and daily advertising spend. [Download the example](./example.json) for a two-campaign comparison. All money fields are nonnegative integers in the smallest unit of the snapshot currency. For USD, `1250` means $12.50.

| Field                     | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `currency`                | One uppercase ISO currency for the whole snapshot.                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| `orders[].id`, `paidAt`   | Unique opaque order ID and UTC payment timestamp with up to millisecond precision. Supply paid merchandise orders after discounts, excluding tax and shipping income.                                                                                  |
| `orders[].sales`, `costs` | Merchandise sales and total variable costs. Costs include product, fulfillment, and payment fees. Supply a cost of `null` when the total is unknown.                                                                                                   |
| `orders[].refunds[]`      | Each merchandise refund has a unique opaque `id` and an `amount`. The report subtracts refunds from the original payment date and keeps the supplied costs.                                                                                            |
| `orders[].clicks[]`       | Observed clicks already linked to this order. Each has an opaque `id`, UTC timestamp `at`, `channel`, `campaign`, and boolean `eligible`. The source sets eligibility according to consent and tracking evidence. IDs must be unique within the order. |
| `spend[]`                 | Daily campaign records with `date` (UTC calendar day), `channel`, `campaign`, and `amount`. One record per day, channel, and campaign. Use consistent channel and campaign names across clicks and spend.                                              |

The signed-in preview accepts `{ snapshot, model, windowDays }`. Choose `first-click` or `last-click` and a 7- or 28-day window. Clicks qualify from exactly that many days before payment through payment itself, inclusive. When eligible clicks share a timestamp, the lexicographically smallest click ID wins. Input order never breaks a tie.

Each order receives one allocation, including its sales, refunds, and costs. The preview returns daily campaign rows, per-order evidence of the chosen click, and matched and total order counts. The reserved **Unmatched** channel collects orders without a qualifying click. Its spend allocation is zero because all supplied ad spend belongs to named campaigns. A missing spend entry for a named campaign remains unknown. A spend-only campaign appears with zero revenue. Unknown costs or spend propagate into the report's contribution calculation.

Use opaque source IDs and keep customer names, addresses, emails, and other personal fields out of imports. The schema rejects extra fields. Each snapshot supports up to 2,000 orders, 5,000 spend records, 32 refunds per order, and 32 clicks per order. Duplicate source records and totals outside the exact integer range produce a validation error.
